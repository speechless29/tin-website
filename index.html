02 · RESEARCH

From one label to 28.

Do LLM Cascade Help Multi-Label Emotion Classification? Strong Verifiers, Weak Proposers

That Lumio limitation became a research question. Instead of forcing one emotion onto a piece of text, I studied multi-label classification across 28 labels, then asked a second question: if a smaller classifier is unsure about a label, does sending that decision to an LLM actually help?

read paper ↗

THE SETUP

A DistilRoBERTa classifier scores all 28 emotions. A label-level uncertainty router looks at each score relative to that label’s decision threshold. Confident decisions stay with Tier 1; uncertain ones can be reviewed by a larger language model, then merged back into the final prediction.

WHAT SURPRISED ME

The expensive second stage wasn’t the main win. Tuning the first-stage decision threshold did almost all of the useful work. On held-out testing, the validation-selected LLM cascade did not produce a reliable improvement.

28emotion labels
5LLMs tested
87.8–94.7%precision when removing a questionable label
18.0–22.9%precision when proposing a missing label

THE SHORT VERSION

The LLMs were much better at checking an existing positive prediction than proposing a new one: strong verifiers, weak proposers.

A validation-tuned global threshold raised macro-F1 from 0.4614 to 0.5270 without Tier 2 calls. The stronger per-label Tier 1 baseline reached 0.5298; the validation-selected cascade scored 0.5256 on the full test split.
